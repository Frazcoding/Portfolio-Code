# Market scan — heated glasshouse / CEA in Scotland and the UK (2026-10-02)

Desk research to sit beside `SCOPE.md`. Covers two kinds of "competition":

1. **Growers** a heated Scottish glasshouse would sell against (the produce market).
2. **Simulation, design and control tools** that do what this model does (if the model itself is the product).

Each figure has a source (listed at the end). Confidence: **sourced** = trade press, company or official figure;
**check** = single source, old, or in conflict with other sources.

---

## 1. Headlines

- **Scotland is nearly empty.** The SAC/SRUC guide (Jan 2026) found **one** commercially heated glasshouse in
  Scotland: Standhill Farm, Hawick. It also found four vertical farms, two of them research units. Scottish CEA
  today is mostly heated or LED-boosted soft-fruit tunnels.
- **UK heated glass is concentrated, old and under cost pressure.** About 2,085 ha of glass, over 70 % of it more
  than 40 years old. Five groups dominate tomatoes and cucumbers. From April 2026 a 94 % rise in transmission
  network charges adds **> £1 M/yr** to some large growers' bills, and horticulture is excluded from the
  energy-intensive industries exemption.
- **New UK capacity is waste-heat and heat-pump led.** These schemes run the same type of plant as this study
  (ammonia heat pumps, low-grade heat sources):
  - AGR Fenland: 33 MW of NH₃ heat pumps plus CHP;
  - Low Carbon Farming: 29 ha heated from Anglian Water wastewater heat.
  They are the closest benchmark for cost and carbon.
- **Vertical farming is a cautionary tale.** Jones Food Company was the UK's biggest vertical farm and entered
  administration in April 2025. GrowUp Farms survives, and the clear difference is its on-site renewable power.
  This matches this study's finding that lit crops are only viable on very cheap electricity.
- **Simulation tools are crowded at the Dutch end** (Hortinergy, Priva ECO, Source.ag, Blue Radix, KASPRO). None
  combines a UK-specific site and tariff model, a refrigerant-cycle heat pump model, wind/grid supply costs and an
  import benchmark. This study combines all four.

**Scottish waste-heat resource.** The ClimateXChange 2020 study ranked **distilleries** and **wastewater treatment**
as Scotland's largest waste-heat sources. A distillery-heated tomato glasshouse was trialled in Scotland in the past,
so the concept has precedent. These sources match this study's "SM" waste-heat option and are the Scottish
equivalent of the Low Carbon Farming / Anglian Water model.

**Takeaway:** in Scotland the competitor for heated tomatoes and cucumbers is essentially one 1.6 ha biomass site
plus imports. The market is open, but the site economics have to work without RHI. The Perthshire off-grid result
(no crop wins on cost) is consistent with why nobody else has built here.

| Grower | Scale | Crops | Energy | Notes |

| **Thanet Earth** (Kent) | ~90 ha site (51 ha glass planned) | Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers | Gas CHP (heat, power, CO₂). New LED lighting for winter cucumbers | Largest UK complex. Sainsbury's 10-year deal |
| **APS Group / APS Salads** | 56.4 ha | Tomatoes (65+ varieties) | Mostly gas/CHP | ~30 % of UK tomato production |

| **Flavourfresh** (Lancashire) | 17.3 ha, 3 sites | Tomatoes, strawberries, blackberries | — | Northern UK grower, nearest climate analogue |
| **Evesham Vale Growers** | — | Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers | — | Sainsbury's 10-year deal |
| **AGR Fenland glasshouse** | 21.7 ha (217,000 m²) | Cucumbers (~10 % of UK demand) | **4 × NH₃ industrial heat pumps, 33 MW thermal** + gas CHP (power + CO₂). ~30 % less CO₂ than conventional | **Direct technical benchmark for this study's two-stage R717 design**; Alfa Laval case study |
| **Low Carbon Farming** (Oasthouse Ventures, Greencoat Capital) — Crown Point (Norwich) + Ingham (Suffolk) | 29 ha combined, £120 M (Ingham) | Tomatoes, hydroponic (~12 % of UK tomatoes) | Heat pumps on Anglian Water wastewater heat, 72 MW captured, 2.5-mile pipe. ESB designs and operates. ~75 % lower emissions | Ingham close to completion, with revised (smaller) plans submitted. 41 further sites identified. Also expanding to Virginia, USA (~$105 M) |

**What this means for the study's cost result.**
- At Chelmsford, tomato comes out at £1.45/kg against an import CIF of £1.60/kg. That is a thin margin against
  incumbents that have scale and CHP, or near-free waste heat in Low Carbon Farming's case.
- A commodity tomato or cucumber from a new heat-pump-on-grid site is a price-taker against these firms.
  Differentiation (Scottish provenance, organic, season, speciality varieties) or a cheap heat source is needed.
- **Model gap:** the incumbents mainly use **gas CHP**. The study benchmarks against imports but not against a UK
  CHP glasshouse. Adding a CHP reference case (using WUR Energiemonitor figures) would show where the heat pump
  stands against the domestic price-setter.

## 6. Headwinds and policy

- **Network charges:** TNUoS rates rose ~94 % from April 2026, adding **> £1 M/yr** for some large glasshouse
  businesses. Horticulture is outside the Energy Intensive Industries exemption because of outdated SIC codes. A
  government review of the exemption list is due in 2026. Electric-heated (heat pump) glasshouses are exposed to
  this far more than gas CHP sites. **Action:** add network charges explicitly in `energy_systems.py`, as the
  DESNZ-scaled Agile tariff may not reflect the 2026 standing charges. Locational TNUoS demand tariffs differ by
  zone, and northern Scotland may be favourable. Check this before the Perthshire grid-backup run.
- **Labour, energy and investment** remain the industry's top three asks (AgNavigator, July 2026).
- **Scottish support:** there is no dedicated CEA or glasshouse scheme.
  - Relevant routes: Future Farming Investment Scheme (round 2, £14.25 M), Agricultural Modernisation (£26 M),
    and HIE support in the Highlands & Islands.
  - Scotland's **producer organisation** route (via the SAOS/SAC networks) is an advantage England and Wales lack.
- **England:** Defra's *Blueprint to grow the UK fruit and vegetable sector* is the policy anchor for the
  self-sufficiency argument.

## 7. Simulation, design and control tools (if the model is the product)

| Tool | Type | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **KASPRO** (WUR) | Research greenhouse climate/energy model | Academic gold standard. Used through WUR consultancy, not sold as a product |
| **Hortinergy** (FR) | Commercial greenhouse energy simulator / "digital twin" (SaaS) | Closest commercial analogue. Sizes heating, cooling, dehumidification and lighting by site and climate |
| **Priva ECO** (NL) | Digital twin + ML energy cost optimiser | Built into Priva climate computers. Operational, not feasibility |
| **Source.ag** (NL) | AI cultivation and yield forecasting. > $60 M raised ($17.5 M Series B, Nov 2025; investors include Enza Zaden) | Crop and yield focus |
| **Blue Radix** (NL) | Autonomous climate control ("Crop Controller"), ~100 greenhouses, €5 M raised | Control, not design |
| **Koidra** (US) | Autonomous greenhouse control, $4.5 M seed | Control |
| Greenhouse builders (Certhon, KUBO, Bridge Greenhouses UK) and energy firms (ESB, AGR, Clarke Energy CHP) | Do in-house design simulation when selling projects | Vertically integrated |

**Gap this study fills.** Most tools are either crop and climate control (Dutch, operational) or generic greenhouse
energy sizing. None ties together all of:
- a refrigerant-cycle heat pump model (two-stage NH₃, single-stage, air/ground/water sources);
- hourly UK weather for any site plus UK tariffs, with real wind and battery costs (PyPSA);
- a UK import benchmark by origin for carbon and cost per kg, across 10 crops;
- Scottish waste-heat options (distillery, wastewater, supermarket, data centre).

**Plausible buyers are feasibility customers, not growers:**
- heat owners looking for an offtaker: data-centre developers (e.g. ILI Group), distilleries, Scottish Water,
  supermarkets;
- funders and advisers: HIE, SAC Consulting, SAOS, Greencoat-type investors;
- new entrants who need a bankable pre-feasibility before engaging a Dutch builder.

That market is small and project-based (consultancy), not a SaaS market.

## 8. Implications for the study (suggested)

1. **Scotland strategy:** go after differentiated produce plus a cheap heat source, not commodity tomatoes on grid
   power.
   - Model a Standhill-style comparator (biomass + biogas CO₂) as the Scottish incumbent.
   - Model a distillery or wastewater heat case in the Carse of Gowrie / Tayside.
2. **Add two benchmarks:**
   - (a) a UK gas-CHP glasshouse, the domestic price-setter;
   - (b) the AGR Fenland / Low Carbon Farming heat-pump reference, the best-in-class new build.
3. **Add 2026 network charges** before the Perthshire grid-backup run, and check the northern Scotland TNUoS zone.
4. **Sales report pricing:** the 10-year supermarket deals show the channel exists. Use DEFRA wholesale as a
   floor and note the Scottish-provenance premium as upside. Standhill's retail pricing could be sampled as
   evidence.
5. **Resolve the self-sufficiency number** (5 % vs ~15–20 %) with Defra *Horticulture Statistics* before it
   appears in any report.
