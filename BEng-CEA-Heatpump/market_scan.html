Produce market

  • Scotland is nearly empty. The competition is one 1.6 ha biomass site, heated soft-fruit tunnels, and imports.
  • UK prices are set by a handful of large growers (Thanet Earth, APS, Green House Growers), most using gas CHP.
  • New UK capacity is heat pumps and waste heat (AGR Fenland, Low Carbon Farming), the same approach as this study.
  • Supermarkets will sign 10-year supply deals (Sainsbury's with Thanet Earth and Evesham Vale), so the sales channel exists.

Simulation tools

  • Dutch tools dominate: Hortinergy, Priva ECO, Source.ag, Blue Radix, and WUR's KASPRO research model.
  • None combines a UK heat-pump cycle model with wind/grid costs and an import benchmark. This study does.
  • Likely buyers want feasibility studies: owners of waste heat, funders and new entrants. That is consultancy work, not SaaS.

Scottish waste heat. ClimateXChange (2020) ranked distilleries and wastewater treatment as Scotland's largest waste-heat sources, and a distillery-heated tomato glasshouse has been trialled before. These are the Scottish equivalent of Low Carbon Farming's Anglian Water model and match the study's waste-heat options.
Takeaway. Scotland is open for heated tomatoes and cucumbers, but a site has to make money without the RHI. The Perthshire off-grid result, where no crop wins on cost, fits with nobody else having built there.

For the cost result. Chelmsford tomato comes out at £1.45/kg against an import price of £1.60/kg. That margin is thin against growers with scale and CHP, or near-free waste heat. A commodity crop from a new grid-powered heat-pump site would be a price-taker; it needs differentiation or cheap heat. Model gap: there is no UK gas-CHP glasshouse reference case yet.

Headwinds and policy

Energy

  • TNUoS rates rose ~94 % from April 2026; some large growers pay > £1 M/yr more.
  • Horticulture is excluded from the Energy Intensive Industries exemption because of outdated SIC codes; a review is due in 2026.
  • Electric heat pumps are hit harder than gas CHP. Action: add network charges explicitly in energy_systems.py and check the northern Scotland TNUoS zone, which may be favourable.

Support

  • No dedicated Scottish CEA or glasshouse scheme.
  • Routes: Future Farming Investment Scheme (round 2, £14.25 M), Agricultural Modernisation (£26 M), HIE in the Highlands & Islands.
  • Scotland's producer organisation route is an advantage England and Wales lack.
  • England: Defra's Blueprint to grow the UK fruit and vegetable sector.
  • Industry's top three asks: labour, energy, investment (July 2026).

The gap this study fills

  • Refrigerant-cycle heat pump model (two-stage NH₃, single-stage; air, ground and water sources)
  • Hourly UK weather for any site, UK tariffs, and real wind and battery costs (PyPSA)
  • UK import benchmark by origin, for carbon and cost per kg, across 10 crops
  • Scottish waste-heat options: distillery, wastewater, supermarket, data centre

Plausible buyers (feasibility, not SaaS)

  • Owners of waste heat looking for a use: data-centre developers (ILI Group), distilleries, Scottish Water, supermarkets
  • Funders and advisers: HIE, SAC Consulting, SAOS, Greencoat-type investors
  • New entrants needing a bankable pre-feasibility before engaging a Dutch builder

Implications for the study

  1. Scotland strategy: differentiated produce plus a cheap heat source, not commodity tomatoes on grid power. Model a Standhill-style biomass + biogas-CO₂ comparator, and a distillery or wastewater heat case in the Carse of Gowrie / Tayside.
  2. Add two reference cases: a UK gas-CHP glasshouse (the domestic price-setter), and the AGR Fenland / Low Carbon Farming heat-pump sites (best new builds).
  3. Add 2026 network charges before the Perthshire grid-backup run, and check the northern Scotland TNUoS zone.
  4. Sales report pricing: the 10-year supermarket deals show the channel exists. Use DEFRA wholesale as the floor and the Scottish-provenance premium as upside; Standhill's retail prices could serve as evidence.
  5. Resolve the self-sufficiency figure (5 % vs ~15–20 %) with Defra Horticulture Statistics before it goes in any report.

Not verified in this scan

  • Current status of Vertical Future, LettUs Grow and Fischer Farms; names of the two north-east Scottish commercial vertical farms.
  • Whether Standhill still runs at full scale in 2026 (most coverage is 2017–2019; RHI closed to new applicants).
  • Hortinergy and Priva pricing; Shockingly Fresh delivery against its 40-site plan.
